J Day Offroad app: Privacy Policy

Scoops Dev LLC · Last updated October 13, 2026

Who we are

The J Day Offroad app ("the app") is a race-day app for riders, families, fans and staff of J Day Offroad ("J Day"), an off-road motorcycle race series. Scoops Dev LLC ("we", "us"), a Vermont limited liability company in the United States, builds the app, its staff panel and the server they use, and runs them for J Day. J Day's staff decide what the app announces and keep J Day's race-day records in the staff panel.

This policy covers the app and the web pages it uses: the shared-link pages on jdayoffroad.pages.dev and J Day's staff panel. It does not cover J Day's own website or Moto-Tally (see "Links to other services").

Summary

  • There are no accounts for riders, families or fans. The app never asks for an email address, phone number or age, and the names you type for the riders you follow stay on your phone.
  • The names and plate numbers of the riders you follow, your settings and your pit stay on your phone. What leaves it is listed below: the series and classes you follow if you turn on Messages, and whatever you choose to share.
  • If you turn on Messages from J Day, your phone's push address and the series and classes you follow are sent to J Day's server, so it can send you messages. Nothing else about you is sent.
  • Your location is used on your phone to show where you are at the venue. It leaves your phone only if you share your pit or copy your position from the Emergency card.
  • No advertising, no analytics, no tracking, and we do not sell or share information for advertising.

What stays on your phone

Everything you set up in the app is stored on your phone only, in the app's own storage: the riders you follow (the name and plate number you type, their series and classes, and which one is you), the classes and series you follow, your alert settings, and your pit at each venue (its place on the map, its GPS position if you saved it with GPS, and your note). Like other app data it is included in your phone's own backups if you make them. It is not sent to us or to J Day, except as described under Messages and Sharing below.

The app also keeps a copy of J Day's season (schedule, results, maps and announcements) and the venue map photos on your phone, so it works with no signal.

The season download

To show the schedule, results, points, venue maps and announcements, the app downloads J Day's season from J Day's server, and checks it for changes while the app is open and when a message from J Day arrives. These requests send nothing about you beyond what every internet request carries: your phone's IP address, and a standard label iOS adds to every request, naming the app, its build number and the phone's system version.

Messages from J Day

Messages are off until you turn them on in the Me tab. When they are on, the app sends J Day's server:

  • your phone's push address, a code Apple gives the app so that notifications can reach this phone. It does not contain your name or phone number;
  • the series and classes you follow, including the classes of the riders you follow, so you get the messages meant for them. Rider names and plate numbers are not sent;
  • the app's version, that the phone is an iPhone, and whether the build uses Apple's test or live push service.

The app sends these again each time it opens and whenever you change what you follow, while Messages are on. J Day's server uses them only to choose which phones a message goes to, and sends each message through Apple's Push Notification service. A message's title and text go to Apple to be delivered, and messages can show on your lock screen.

When you turn Messages off, the app asks the server to delete your phone's push address and what you follow, at once. If the phone has no signal, it asks again the next time the app opens. If Apple tells us an address no longer works (for example, the app was deleted), we stop sending to it. Any push address the app has not sent again for 180 days is deleted automatically, once a week.

Staging alerts

Staging alerts are reminders set on your phone, before the motos of the riders and classes you follow. They are scheduled by the app on your phone and involve no server.

Location

With your permission, and only while you are using the app, it uses your phone's location to show where you are on the venue map, how far you are from your pit, first aid and other places, to point an arrow to them, and to save your pit's position if you choose "I'm at my pit". The app uses location only while a venue map, the Spectator guide, the Emergency card or the arrow is on screen, and forgets the position when they close.

Your location is not sent to us or to J Day. It leaves your phone only when you choose to:

  • Share your pit. The pit link and message carry your pit's GPS position, if you saved it with GPS, and a walking-directions link to Apple Maps with that position.
  • Copy from the Emergency card. The text you copy includes your current GPS position, with how accurate it is and the time, to read to a dispatcher or paste into a message.

The Satellite view shows Apple Maps imagery of the venue, loaded from Apple. You can change or remove location access at any time in iOS Settings → J Day Offroad → Location.

Sharing riders and your pit

You can share the riders you follow (as a link or a QR code) or your pit (as a link). Everything shared travels inside the link itself, after the "#": for riders, their names, plate numbers, series and classes, and the classes you watch (not which rider is you); for a pit, the venue, its place on the map, its GPS position if saved, and your note. Anyone who has the link can read what is in it, so share it only with people you trust.

A shared link opens a page on jdayoffroad.pages.dev, or the app if it is installed. Browsers never send the part of a link after the "#" to a website, so what is in the link does not reach that site or us. The pages read it in your browser only; they have no forms, cookies, analytics or third-party code, and ask search engines not to list them.

Results, points and announcements are public

The season J Day publishes in the app can be downloaded by anyone, with or without the app. It includes the schedule, venue maps and notices, announcements and messages from J Day staff, and the published results and points, which list each rider's name, plate number, class, finishing position or status (such as DNF), laps, points and any note staff add to a result.

The official results and points are J Day's, on Moto-Tally; the app shows a copy. To ask about a published result, contact J Day.

J Day's staff panel

J Day's staff use a password-protected web panel to run events. It holds information that only signed-in staff can read:

  • Staff logins. Each staff member's email address, password (kept by the sign-in service in a form we cannot read), name and role. An administrator creates each login.
  • Race-day roster and check-in. The riders entered at each event, imported by staff from J Day's pre-entry list or a spreadsheet, or added at the sign-up table: name, plate number in each series, classes, and, when the list has them, hometown, bike and email address; and whether and when each rider checked in, and which staff login checked them in. Staff can download these lists as spreadsheets.
  • Rider registrations. A list of rider registrations that early test versions of the app could send (name, plate number, series, classes, and optionally hometown, bike and email). The app no longer sends them and nobody else can add to the list.
  • Announcements. Each announcement records which staff login created it, as a random ID that does not name anyone. That ID can be read along with the public announcement.

The rider app never sees the roster or check-ins. The panel loads its fonts from Google Fonts, and its map tools load aerial photos of venues from the U.S. Geological Survey; those requests carry the staff member's IP address but no rider or staff information.

Where information is kept, and who handles it

  • Supabase hosts J Day's server and database (the season, push addresses, the staff panel's records and staff logins) in its United States East region (Amazon Web Services, northern Virginia).
  • Apple delivers messages through its Push Notification service, provides the Satellite view's imagery and Apple Maps directions, and provides the app through the App Store and TestFlight, under Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • Cloudflare hosts the shared-link pages (jdayoffroad.pages.dev) and the staff panel (jdayoffroad-staff.pages.dev).
  • GitHub (owned by Microsoft) keeps a nightly backup copy of the whole database, including the roster, check-ins and staff logins, as a private file in our private code repository, deleted automatically after 30 days. It is never published. A backup can be restored into a temporary database to check it works; that database is deleted when the check ends.

Like any web service, Supabase, Cloudflare, Google and GitHub see the IP address of the phone or browser that connects to them, and may keep it in their own logs under their own privacy policies. We do not use those logs to identify anyone. Information is stored and handled in the United States.

Links to other services

The app links to Moto-Tally, which J Day uses for pre-entry, series membership, scoring and official results; to pages J Day adds to an event (such as a waiver, photos or a live broadcast); and to Apple Maps for directions. These open in your browser or in Apple Maps. The app sends nothing to them; what you enter there is covered by their own policies.

What we do not do

  • No accounts for riders, families or fans, and no sign-in in the app.
  • No advertising, analytics, crash-reporting or tracking code, and no third-party code at all in the app.
  • We do not sell personal information, or share it with anyone for advertising or for their own purposes.

Children and families

The app is for a general audience: racers of every age race in J Day's series, including youth classes, and families follow them. It is not directed to children and does not ask anyone's age. The app does not ask anyone for contact details, and the riders you follow stay on your phone.

If a child uses the app, the only information that can leave the phone is what is described above: with Messages on, the push address and the series and classes followed; and whatever the child chooses to share. A parent or guardian who wants information about their child deleted can turn Messages off on the child's phone (which deletes the push address at once), delete the app, or email us at the address below. We will delete what we hold about that child, and pass the request to J Day for its own records. Results are J Day's official records; a request about a published result is something we pass to J Day.

Your choices, access and deletion

  • On your phone: edit or remove the riders, classes and pits you follow at any time in the app; deleting the app removes everything it kept on your phone.
  • Messages: turn them off in the Me tab, which deletes your push address and what you follow from J Day's server; or turn off notifications for the app in iOS Settings.
  • Location: change it in iOS Settings → J Day Offroad → Location.
  • Records about you in the staff panel (a roster entry, a check-in, a staff login): email us to see, correct or delete them. We reply within 30 days. A deleted record stays in the nightly backups until they are deleted, within 30 days.

Because riders, fans and families have no accounts, we cannot link a push address to a person. To have one deleted, turn Messages off in the app.

Canada

If you are in Canada, you can ask to see, correct or delete personal information about you, and to know how it is handled, by writing to our privacy contact below. We reply within 30 days. Information is stored and handled in the United States, as described above, where it may be accessible to the courts and authorities there.

Changes to this policy

When the app or what it handles changes, we update this page and the date at the top.
